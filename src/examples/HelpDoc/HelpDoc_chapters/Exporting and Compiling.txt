[>c] # Exporting and Compiling

WareWoolf uses “Export” to mean exporting each chapter and your notes individually as their own files, while “Compile” exports everything joined together in one file.

# Export Options
There are several file formats currently supported for export: .docx, .txt, .mdfc (which is the extension for MarkupFic text files), .md (normal Markdown), .html, and .epub.

You can also tick “Mark scene breaks with a centered #”, described under Compile Options below. On export it applies to your chapters only, not to your notes or the corkboard.

# Compile Options
In addition to filetype, you can choose whether to insert a string between each chapter (useful if you want to mark chapter breaks in plaintext file) and you can have WareWoolf insert your Chapter Titles as headings. (“Chapter Titles” here means the label you gave your chapters in the list on the left in WareWoolf, not any titles you may have written within each chapter itself. So if, like me, you are in the habit of writing “Chapter One” at the beginning of each chapter, there would be no need to use this function.)

If compiling to .docx, .html, or .epub, you can also automatically generate a title page with word count, etc. This will pull your address info, if any, from the “Settings” page.

# Marking Scene Breaks
Standard manuscript format marks the break between one scene and the next with a centered “#” on the blank line between them, so that a reader can tell a deliberate break from a page break that happened to fall in the same place. Ticking “Mark scene breaks with a centered #” writes that character into the compiled or exported document for you. Your own chapters are not touched — the mark is added to the copy on its way into the output file, so the blank lines in your manuscript stay blank lines.

Only a blank line with an ordinary paragraph both above it and below it is marked. That rules out the blank line under a chapter heading, the blank lines you may have left trailing at the end of a chapter, and any gap beside a heading, a list, a block quotation or a footnote. Every blank line in a wider gap is marked, so if you separate your scenes with two blank lines you will get two hashes — Tools > Tab-Indent Paragraphs, with “Remove Blank Lines Between Paragraphs” ticked, is what closes those up.

The box applies to every file format, but only the ones that carry alignment (.docx, .mdfc, .html and .epub) can center the mark. In .txt and .md, which have no alignment of their own, the “#” is written at the left margin.
