[>c] # Footnotes

WareWoolf has first-class footnotes: a numbered marker in your text, and the note itself at the bottom of the chapter, kept in step with each other so that you never have to number anything yourself.

# Adding a Note
Put the caret where you want the marker and press Ctrl+Alt+F (Cmd+Alt+F on Mac). That drops in a numbered marker and jumps you straight into an empty note at the bottom of the chapter, ready to type. It is one action, and one step to undo.

# Moving Between a Marker and Its Note
Ctrl+Alt+F does double duty as a jump:
* with the caret just before a marker, it jumps to that note;
* from inside a note, it jumps back to its marker.

Enter does the same forward jump when the caret sits just before a marker. Wherever the caret is, a marker and its note are highlighted together, so you can always see which note you are in.

# Numbering
Numbers are assigned and reassigned for you. Add a note in the middle of a chapter and the ones after it renumber; delete one and the rest close up; move a paragraph and its notes reorder to match the text. You never type a number yourself.

Deleting a marker deletes its note along with it, as a single undo step, so a note is never left stranded at the bottom of a chapter with nothing pointing at it.

# Multiple Paragraphs
Just keep typing. Pressing Enter inside a note starts a new paragraph of the same note, rather than ending it.

# Copying and Pasting
Copying or cutting a marker carries its note along with it, so pasting a passage into another chapter brings its footnotes too. The pasted notes are renumbered to suit their new home.

# Splitting a Chapter
A marker and its note always end up on the same side of a split, wherever the marker went. The one thing WareWoolf will not do is split a chapter inside a note itself; it asks you to move the caret out of the note first, rather than guess where you meant the break to fall.

# Compiling and Exporting
Compiling a whole project numbers every chapter’s notes into one sequence, so a five-chapter book does not come out with five notes numbered 1.

Notes then survive export in whatever form the format has for them: real Word footnotes in .docx, linked notes in HTML and EPUB, and \[^1] markers in Markdown and MarkdownFic. That last is a syntax you can also write by hand — see the MarkdownFic chapter — and a file written that way reads back in as real footnotes.
